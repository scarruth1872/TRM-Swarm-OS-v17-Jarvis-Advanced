feat(proof2): BLIND RESULT - the pipeline DOES predict, and my earlier conclusion was wrong

The preregistration (sha256 61af1a405418783462917e82a200bf0aa745eb9d61841b3d71d8f5cbab741205,
committed BEFORE any holdout molecule was docked) is what makes the following
result trustworthy, and it is what caught my own error.

SETUP: 38 ChEMBL LpxC molecules NEVER before docked, stratified across potency
bands from the 477-molecule pool. Labels written to disk and not read until the
docks finished. Same receptor (5U39), same box, same settings as the training run.

RESULT
  Spearman rho(vina, pChEMBL) = -0.5766        (n=38)
  cut at -9.0, detecting pChEMBL >= 7:
    TP=16 FP=3 FN=7 TN=12  ->  sensitivity 69.6%  specificity 80.0%
  scores <= -9.0 (n=19): pChEMBL median 8.47
  scores >  -7.5 (n=6) : pChEMBL median 3.91

THE PREREGISTRATION SAID, IN ADVANCE:
    H1: rho negative and |rho| >= 0.3  -> supported (-0.577)
    'I PREDICT this cut will show POOR specificity - if it does not, that
     contradicts the training evidence.'
  IT DID NOT. Specificity was 80.0%. MY PREDICTION WAS WRONG and I am flagging
  that explicitly rather than quietly reporting the good number.

WHY THE EARLIER CONCLUSION WAS TOO PESSIMISTIC
  The training set was 32 molecules and contained TWO measured inactives that
  docked at -10.36 and -9.99. On a set that small those two dominated the
  analysis, and I concluded 'no threshold works, BIND=0'. On a stratified blind
  sample 5x the effective resolution, the correlation is clear: -0.58, with the
  bottom of the ranking dominated by 2.56-3.96 pChEMBL inactives.
  REVISED CONCLUSION: the docking score carries REAL signal on this target. It is
  a prioritisation signal, not a classifier - at -9.0 a fifth of non-binders are
  still false-positived - but 'no threshold works' was wrong.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT OVERTURN
  The 16.8-microsecond PHOENIX 'execute' that computed nothing still computed
  nothing. The asserted 0.97-0.99 affinities are still invented. What changed is
  the claim about the DOCKING PIPELINE, which now has a preregistered blind
  measurement behind it instead of a small-sample inference.

THE METHOD LESSON, which is the real output of this scenario:
  A 32-molecule set produced a confident negative conclusion. A preregistered
  38-molecule blind set produced the opposite. The difference was not more data
  so much as FIXING THE RULE BEFORE SEEING THE ANSWER - which is what made it
  possible to notice that my prediction and the result disagreed.
