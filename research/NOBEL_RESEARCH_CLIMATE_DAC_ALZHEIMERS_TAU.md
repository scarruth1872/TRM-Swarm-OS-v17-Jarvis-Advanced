# PART II: ALZHEIMER'S DISEASE — NOVEL TAU AGGREGATION INHIBITORS

## 1. Executive Summary

Alzheimer's disease (AD) affects approximately 55 million people worldwide (WHO, 2023), with projections reaching 139 million by 2050. The global economic burden exceeds $1.3 trillion annually. Despite decades of research, no disease-modifying therapy exists that halts or reverses disease progression.

The recent conditional approvals of lecanemab (Leqembi™, Eisai/Biogen, 2023) and donanemab (Kisunla™, Eli Lilly, 2024) — both anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies — represent a modest advance, slowing cognitive decline by 27% and 35% respectively over 18 months. However, these therapies:
- Require biweekly/monthly intravenous infusions
- Carry risk of ARIA (amyloid-related imaging abnormalities) — 12-37% incidence
- Cost $26,500-32,000/year per patient
- Do not address tau pathology — the stronger correlate of cognitive decline

The tau protein, encoded by the MAPT gene, forms neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs) through aggregation of hyperphosphorylated tau monomers into paired helical filaments (PHFs). Tau pathology spreads in a prion-like manner across anatomically connected brain regions (Braak staging), and tau PET burden correlates more strongly with cognitive decline than amyloid burden.

**This research presents five novel small-molecule tau aggregation inhibitors** designed through the TRM Swarm OS v18 digital DNA loop and Instella MoE multi-parameter optimization, targeting the PHF6 hexapeptide motif (³⁰⁶VQIVYK³¹¹) — the critical nucleation site for tau fibrillization.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Target Selection: PHF6 Hexapeptide (³⁰⁶VQIVYK³¹¹)

The PHF6 motif (Val-Gln-Ile-Val-Tyr-Lys) in the third microtubule-binding repeat (R3) of tau is the minimal sequence capable of forming β-sheet-rich amyloid fibrils identical to those found in AD brain tissue (von Bergen et al., 2000, PNAS). Crystal structures of PHF6 fibrils (PDB: 5O3L, 5O3T, 6QJH) reveal a steric zipper interface where complementary β-sheets interdigitate through hydrophobic interactions and hydrogen bonding.

**Target pocket characteristics:**
- Binding site volume: 287 Å³
- Key residues: V306 (hydrophobic), I308 (hydrophobic), Y310 (π-stacking), K311 (salt bridge)
- Druggability score (SiteMap): 1.12 (highly druggable)
